WHICH WATER IS THIS?The PRIMARY COOLANT. Yellow: hot-leg coolant leaving the core; cyan: cold-leg coolant returning from the steam generator (about 280 °C in this model). The primary and secondary loops are separate. The saturation boundary uses the Wagner–Pruss water-vapor-pressure correlation; saturation temperature is obtained by numerical inversion. This is a saturation-line calculation, not a full water-property solver or plant operating chart.
1 · SUBCOOLED LIQUIDBelow the saturation temperature at the current pressure, the primary coolant remains liquid. The illustrative reference point is about 315 °C and 15.5 MPa. The subcooling margin is Tsat(P) − T. The model uses a water-vapor-pressure correlation for the saturation boundary; it does not calculate full steam-table properties.
2 · WATER ABOVE ITS CRITICAL POINTAt about 373.946 °C and 22.064 MPa, water reaches its critical point. Above both critical coordinates there is no distinct liquid–vapor phase boundary. This thermodynamic condition does not by itself mean a reactor has failed; heat transfer and safety depend on system design and operating conditions.
MODEL LIMITSThis phase map tracks bulk primary coolant only. It does not calculate fuel temperature, cladding temperature, local boiling crisis, departure from nucleate boiling ratio, or vessel stress. The relief-valve and pressure-limit values are game parameters, not universal PWR setpoints.
Supercritical water is a fluid state, not plasma and not a fusion condition. Nuclear fusion is a separate topic and is not represented by this reactor model.
