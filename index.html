      ABOUT PWR SIMULATION

      v2.2.2
      ⚠ NOT FOR REAL PLANT OPERATION — All numbers are classroom parameters. Not validated against any real reactor. Educational use only.
      OVERVIEW

      A simplified, pressurized-water-reactor teaching model. Four crews, one reactor. It teaches reactor dynamics through four control divisions (rods, coolant pump, pressurizer, boron) and, in Advanced Mode, five additional nuclear instrumentation readouts (neutron count rate, dose rate, reactor period, reactivity, thermal flux).

      QUICK START
      • Group Mode — projector + up to four phones. Host runs the reactor, students control divisions.
      • Sandbox Mode — single screen, all controls. No timer, no win condition.
      • Learning Mode — clickable reactor schematic. Explore components and physics.
      • Advanced Mode — nuclear instrumentation, CSV data recorder, and (planned) guided experiments.
      OBJECTIVES
      • Understand how the four control divisions affect reactor power.
      • Read and interpret hot-leg temperature, system pressure, and grid power.
      • Recognize criticality, subcriticality, and supercriticality via k_eff.
      • Coordinate across divisions to reach and hold a target power level.
      • Practice operating within safe limits and recognize failure modes.
      • (Advanced) Read nuclear instrumentation: count rate, dose, period, reactivity.
      • (Advanced, planned) Perform guided experiments: rod calibration, delayed neutron counting, xenon observation.
      LIMITATIONS
      1. Prompt neutron generation time stretched 1000× for pacing (Λ = 0.1 s vs real ~1e-4 s). Distorts short periods and inflates asymptotic reactivity-vs-period by ~2.18×.
      2. Boron worth realistic (~−10 pcm/ppm, comparable to real PWRs). Doppler coefficients 2–6 pcm/°C depending on scenario — Easy slightly above real, Moderate/Hard in range.
      3. Xenon model distorted: uneven compression (~570× for I-135, ~96× for Xe-135), post-SCRAM peak ~1–2 min with ~17% overshoot, absolute worth ~3–4× real. Full detail in about.html §4.3 and §5.
      4. No spatial dimension. Flux, dose, and power are core-averages, not spatial distributions.
      5. Single cps detector covers all power ranges (real reactors use SR/IR/PR instruments).
      6. Dose assumes a fictional shielded position near primary piping; real detector positions vary by orders of magnitude.
      7. Fission product pool is a single bucket, not an isotope-by-isotope inventory.
      8. Activation products (Co-60, Fe-59) not modeled — real plants retain residual dose for years.
      9. Fixed 20 MWth residual heat allowance at zero power.
      10. 33% thermal-to-electric conversion; 5% station service (real plants vary).
      11. No departure from nucleate boiling (DNB), critical heat flux, or DNBR.
      12. All warning, hold, and failure limits are game parameters — not real plant settings.
      DISCLAIMERS

      This is an educational model, not a validated plant simulator. It is not suitable for real-plant operation, operator training, licensing, or safety analysis. All numbers are illustrative. Dose values assume a fictional detector position and are not representative of any actual installation. The project is provided under the GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 with no warranty.
